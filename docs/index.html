How to use Inkstone

Everything the editor does, in the order you will want it. It takes a few minutes to learn and nothing needs setting up.

Getting started

Open the editor with Start drawing. There is no account and nothing to install: your map is saved in this browser as you work, so it is still there when you come back.

  1. Pick Room in the tool dock at the bottom and drag on the map to draw a room.
  2. Pick Token and click to drop a token. Drag outward while placing it to make it larger.
  3. Click the title at the top to name your map.
  4. Press Share (top right) and send the link to your table, and everyone draws on the same map.

Scroll to zoom, hold Alt and drag (or use the middle mouse button) to pan, and press F to fit the whole map on screen.

The tools

The tool dock holds seven tools. Each has a key, shown in its tooltip.

ToolKeyWhat it does
Select and moveVClick to select, drag to move, drag on empty map to select everything inside a box. Hold Shift to add to a selection.
RoomRDrag out a rectangle. It snaps to the grid, and its width and height are shown as you draw.
WallWDrag a straight wall between two grid points.
TokenTClick to place a token, or drag outward to make it larger.
LabelLClick to write text on the map, or drag an area to set how big it is.
EraseEClick or drag over cells. A wall is removed cell by cell, and everything else as a whole.
MeasureMDrag a ruler between two points to read the distance.

With a room or wall tool, the panel beside the dock sets the outline color, fill color and line thickness. Select a room or wall and drag its handles to resize it, or the round handle above a room to rotate it. Rotation snaps to 15 degrees; hold Shift to turn freely.

Tokens and conditions

Tokens are placed plain. Click one with the select tool and its card appears above it:

  • Name: type in the card, or press Enter or double-click the token. Enter keeps it and Esc puts the old one back. The bold, italic and plate buttons style the name.
  • Color: one of eight swatches, or the ring for any color.
  • Picture: choose a picture and it is cropped to the disc. The color becomes the outline.
  • Conditions: press Add and type to find one (Prone, Poisoned, Dead and the rest of the 5e conditions). They show as round badges on the token, and Dead greys it out and crosses it. Hover a token to read its conditions in words.

Sizes snap to whole squares: one for Medium, two for Large, three for Huge. A copy of a token named Goblin 1 becomes Goblin 2.

You can make your own conditions in Settings → Conditions, with your own name, color and icon.

Labels

With the label tool, click the map to start typing, or drag out an area: the label's text is as tall as the area. Enter or clicking away keeps it, and Esc throws it away. Select a label to change its size and color in its card, and turn on a plate behind it so it reads over a busy map. Double-click a label to edit its text.

Measuring

Press M and drag. The ruler snaps to the corners and middles of squares, and stays up until you click elsewhere. Rooms and walls also show their size while you draw them.

In Settings → Board you choose the unit (feet, meters or squares) and how much one square is. D&D diagonal rules are on by default: the first diagonal square counts as 1, the next as 2, then 1, 2 and so on, as in the Dungeon Master's Guide. Turn it off to measure the true straight line.

Map background

Right-click an empty part of the map to set what is behind the grid:

  • Add image puts a scanned or generated map behind the grid. Move it by dragging, resize it from a corner (its proportions are kept), and hold Shift to snap to the grid. The arrow keys nudge it a pixel at a time.
  • Fit to grid finds the squares printed on the picture and sizes and moves it so they line up with ours. If it can't find a grid it changes nothing and says so.
  • Background color changes the color of the map itself.

While you adjust a picture it is the only thing you can touch, so nothing else moves by accident. Press Done when it is right.

Selecting and editing

  • Move by dragging, or nudge a square at a time with the arrow keys.
  • Copy, paste and duplicate with Ctrl C, V and D. Paste lands at the pointer.
  • Undo and redo with Ctrl Z and Ctrl Shift Z. In a shared map, undo only undoes your last change.
  • Order: right-click an element for Bring to Front and Send to Back.
  • Lock an element from the same menu to turn it into a backdrop that clicks, drags and the eraser pass through. Right-click it to unlock.

Sharing live

Press Share and copy the link. Anyone who opens it joins the same map, and you see each other's changes and pointers as they happen. The first time, you are asked what to call yourself; change it later in Settings → Profile. To join a map you were sent a code for, use Join.

  • Two players editing different things never collide. If you both change the same thing at once, the first change wins and you are told.
  • If your connection drops, Inkstone reconnects by itself. What you did meanwhile is sent when it is back, unless someone changed the same thing first.
  • The map is kept for you: open the link later, even after everyone has left, and it is as you left it. A shared map that no one visits for a week is deleted.

The library

The open-book button under the logo opens the library: sample maps you can start from, such as a castle, a tavern and a road ambush. On a new, empty map the welcome points to it. Choose a card and it opens as a new map of its own, with its tokens and pictures; the map you were on is kept.

My Maps is the first section, above a line that sets it apart from the library's own types. It holds every map you have in this browser: the one on the board (marked On the board now) and the ones you have put away, each with a small picture of it and when you left it. New map starts an empty one; choosing a card moves to that map, putting the one you were on away; Delete on a card (it shows when you point at it) forgets a map for good, after asking. A map with no name and nothing on it is never kept. If the board is shared live, moving to another map asks first, because it leaves the session. The search looks at your maps' names too.

Find one with the search, which looks at names, descriptions and authors. Models and Tokens are marked Soon: the library is where players will share them with each other.

Saving and exporting

  • Automatic: your map is saved in this browser after every change. The small mark at the top right shows when it has been.
  • Save to a file writes a .inkstone.json file you can keep, send or move to another computer, and Open reads one back (as one undo step).
  • Export as PNG makes a picture of the map, named after your map.

Touch screens

Pinch to zoom and drag with two fingers to pan. Press and hold on the map to open the menu you would get from a right-click. Everything else works as it does with a mouse.

Good to know

Is it really free?
Yes. There are no ads, accounts or paywalls. If you want to chip in, there is a "Buy me a coffee" link at the bottom of this page and in Settings.
Where is my map stored?
In your browser, on your computer. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you press Share, and then only to the people with the link. Clearing your browser's site data removes the map, so use Save to a file for anything you want to keep safe.
Can anyone with the link edit my map?
Yes. A shared link lets anyone who has it edit, and names are only labels that nobody checks. Share it with people you trust.
Does it work offline?
Drawing does once the page has loaded. Sharing needs a connection.
Something is wrong or missing.
The "What's new" button in the editor lists what changed lately. A contact page is coming.
